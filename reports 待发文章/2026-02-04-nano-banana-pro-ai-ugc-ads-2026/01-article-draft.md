## The 90‑second answer (AIDA opening)
If your AI UGC ads look “fake,” it’s usually not the script—it’s the *visual cues* (camera feel, skin texture, hands, and product lighting).

In 2026, “native” matters: TikTok reports 71% of users prefer brand posts that don’t feel overly polished. ([TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/quicktok/online/5_creative_tips/pc/en))

The fastest fix is a realism‑first workflow: generate a believable **phone selfie actor** with Nano Banana Pro, build **clean product references**, composite **product‑in‑hand** correctly, then turn *one good frame* into a short talking clip.

From there, add a motion end card and ship variations (hooks, captions, CTAs) to learn faster than you can with traditional filming.

## Definitions (so your prompts stay consistent)
These terms show up throughout the workflow:

- **UGC ad**: A short ad that *feels like a real person filmed it*—casual framing, human delivery, proof‑first beats.
- **Angle**: The single idea you’re selling (pain‑killer, before/after, myth‑bust, comparison, speed).
- **Proof**: The one moment that makes the claim believable (demo, result, side‑by‑side, artifact).
- **Product‑in‑hand demo**: A UGC pattern where the product is clearly shown *in the creator’s hand* while they speak.
- **End card**: The final branded screen (offer + CTA) that makes the ad “finish cleanly.”

## The repeatable workflow (overview)
You’ll run the same steps every time:

1) Pick **one** angle + write **one** script  
2) Generate an **actor selfie image** (Nano Banana Pro)  
3) Build a **product reference pack** (multi‑angle)  
4) Create a **product‑in‑hand composite** (lighting + occlusion)  
5) Generate a **talking clip** (image → video + voice)  
6) Generate a **motion end card**  
7) Assemble, caption, and ship **3–6 variations**

## Step 1 — Choose ONE angle (then write a 20–35s script)
This is where most “AI ads” fail: stacking five messages in one clip.

### Angle menu (pick one)
- **Pain‑killer**: “Stop doing X the hard way…”
- **Myth‑bust**: “Everyone says X. That’s wrong…”
- **Before/after**: “I tried this for 7 days…”
- **Speed**: “Do this in 5 minutes…”
- **Comparison**: “I tested A vs B…”

### Script template (copy/paste)
Keep each block short:

```text
Hook (0–2s): [1 bold line]
Context (2–5s): I’m [who], and I tried [product] because [problem].
Proof (5–18s): [one proof moment: demo/result/side-by-side]
Mechanism (18–25s): It works because [one mechanism].
CTA (last 2–4s): Do [one action] to get [one benefit].
```

### Prompt (to generate hook variants)
```text
Generate 12 UGC hooks for this angle:
- Product: [product]
- Persona: [who is speaking]
- Audience pain: [pain]
- Proof moment: [proof]
Hook constraint: include ONE specific constraint — time, cost, niche, or “without X”.
Constraint rule: each hook must include time/cost/niche/without-X.
Rules: 6–10 words per hook, conversational, no hype claims.
Output: numbered list + 3 “soft” hooks + 3 “bold” hooks.
```

Phone selfie, front camera, natural window light, casual UGC vibe.

- [ ] **Lighting direction is obvious** (you’ll need it for product shadows).

## Step 3 — Build a product reference pack (multi‑angle)
Product‑in‑hand composites hallucinate when the model only sees one angle. Give it a clean, consistent reference pack first.

### Option A (best): real product photos
Shoot 8–12 photos on a table near a window: front, 45°, side, back, top, label close‑up. Keep lighting consistent.

### Option B: generate a product angle grid (copy/paste prompt)
Use one high‑quality product photo as a reference, then:

```text
Create a 3x3 grid of this product from different realistic angles.
Keep logo/label text consistent and readable.
Neutral studio lighting, sharp focus, accurate geometry.
Background: plain light gray.
```

### Product reference sanity check
- [ ] Label stays consistent across angles (no letter drift)
- [ ] Reflections/shadows match a single lighting direction
- [ ] Product proportions don’t “inflate”

## Step 4 — Create the product‑in‑hand composite (the realism bottleneck)
This is the make-or-break step. You’re matching **geometry**, **lighting**, and **occlusion**.

### Composite prompt template (copy/paste)
```text
Using the actor selfie image as the base:
Place the product naturally in the actor’s [left/right] hand.
Match the product scale to the hand and camera perspective.
Lighting: match the original light direction; add realistic contact shadows.
Occlusion: fingers should overlap the product naturally; no floating.
Preserve the actor’s face, skin texture, and background.
Negative: floating product, wrong scale, broken fingers, extra fingers, blurry label, mismatched shadows.
```

### Product‑in‑hand quality checklist
- [ ] **Contact shadow** exists where product touches fingers/palm
- [ ] **Finger occlusion** looks natural (fingers overlap product edges)
- [ ] **Perspective** matches the phone selfie (no “product pasted on”)
- [ ] **Label clarity** is readable at feed size (not tiny, not warped)
- [ ] **Specular highlights** match the original lighting direction

<!-- CITABLE_BLOCK: Product-in-hand success criteria -->
A believable “product‑in‑hand” frame needs four things: correct scale (fits the hand), correct lighting direction (highlights + shadows agree), correct occlusion (fingers overlap edges), and a readable label (no warping). If any one is missing, viewers read it as “pasted” even if the face looks real.

## Step 5 — Generate the talking clip (image → video + voice)
Once you have one great composite frame, generate a **6–10s** talking beat first. Longer clips are harder to keep stable.

### Talking clip prompt template (copy/paste)
```text
Generate a 6–10 second vertical talking-head UGC clip.
Subject: the person speaks to camera naturally (subtle head motion, eye blinks).
Hands: keep the product visible and stable in-hand (no morphing).
Camera: handheld phone selfie feel, slight micro-shake.
Audio: [voice style], clear but not studio-polished.
Script: “[one short line from the hook/context]”
Negative: face melting, hand warping, product changing shape, over-smooth skin.
```

### Voice options (choose one)
- **TTS** (fastest): write a short, conversational line with pauses and breaths.
- **Recorded VO** (highest trust): record on your phone; keep room tone.

## Step 6 — Create a motion end card (don’t try to make it “more UGC”)
End cards perform because they’re clear. Treat it like lightweight motion design:

### End card spec
- 1 headline (benefit)
- 1 proof cue (short)
- 1 offer line (optional)
- 1 CTA (single action)

### End card prompt template (copy/paste)
```text
Create a 2–3 second vertical motion end card for a UGC ad.
Design: clean modern layout, large readable headline, high contrast.
Elements: [brand name], [benefit], [offer], [cta].
Style: minimal motion design, smooth transitions, not “filmed”.
Background: subtle green gradient with lots of negative space.
```

## Step 7 — Assemble and ship 3–6 variations (your real advantage)
Don’t spend 3 hours perfecting one ad. Ship a small variation set:

### Variation matrix (copy/paste)
```text
Generate 6 ad variants from this base script.
Vary only ONE element per variant:
- Variant 1–2: hook change
- Variant 3–4: proof framing change (same proof, different wording)
- Variant 5: CTA wording change
- Variant 6: caption overlay change
Keep claims consistent. Keep length within 20–30s.
Output: table with Variant #, Hook, Proof line, CTA, On-screen caption.
```

### Assembly checklist
- [ ] Captions on (native style, readable)
- [ ] Proof moment appears within the first 8–10 seconds
- [ ] End card shows brand + CTA clearly
- [ ] Export per placement (TikTok/Reels/Shorts) with safe margins

## Nano Banana Pro realism checklist (fast troubleshooting)
Use this table to diagnose what to fix *before* regenerating everything.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fast fix |
|---|---|---|
| Face looks real, hands look wrong | hands under‑specified | “Hands: both hands visible, natural fingers” + strong negatives |
| Skin looks plastic | beauty filter bias | add “natural skin texture, pores, no beauty filter” |
| Product looks pasted | missing shadow/occlusion | explicitly request “contact shadow + finger occlusion” |
| Label warps | label not constrained | generate a clean product ref pack first, then composite |
| Clip morphs product | too long / unstable motion | shorten to 6–10s; prioritize stability over drama |

## Beginner mistakes (and quick fixes)
1) **Your hook is too vague** → Add ONE constraint: time, cost, niche, or “without X” (e.g., “in 10 minutes”, “under $50”, “for Shopify skincare”, “without filming”).  
2) **You’re trying to sell too many features** → Cut down to **one promise + one proof** per clip.  
3) **Proof shows up too late** → Move the demo/result into the first **8–10 seconds**.  
4) **Captions are hard to read + CTA is generic** → Use fewer words, bigger text, higher contrast; make the CTA match the angle (“Get templates” beats “Learn more”).  

## Pro tips (small tweaks that add “human” fast)
- Add **micro‑imperfections**: slight handheld shake, small head turns, natural pauses.
- Keep audio “real”: room tone, tiny breaths, no studio compressor sound.
- Write captions like a creator: short, specific, no brand brochure tone.
- Track assets and approvals: keep the base actor, product refs, and scripts versioned per campaign.

## FAQ

### 1) How long should UGC ads be in 2026?
If you’re new, start with **15–25 seconds**. Shorter ads make it easier to keep the hook + proof + CTA clear—then go longer once you can deliver that consistently.

### 2) Do I have to show my face?
Not necessarily, but face-on camera is the fastest trust builder. If you can’t show your face, use stronger on-screen text and a tighter demo.

### 3) What makes a UGC ad feel “native”?
Natural language, quick context, and specific proof. Avoid overproduced lighting and corporate slogans—clarity + real-sounding audio usually wins.

### 4) Does AI-generated UGC work?
AI works well as **UGC-style** creative—especially for variations and B-roll. Don’t use AI to invent results, reviews, or claims you can’t support.

### 5) What’s the simplest way to write better hooks?
Write 10 hooks for the same angle, then read them out loud. Keep the 2 that sound most like something you’d text a friend.

### 6) What should I measure first?
Start with **retention/completion** (are people still watching after the first few seconds?) and click signals (CTR). Fix the hook first, then optimize everything else.
