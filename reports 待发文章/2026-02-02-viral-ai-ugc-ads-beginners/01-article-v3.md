## Step 0 — Choose one angle (the kill shot for beginners)
Newbie biggest error: stacking five creative ideas at once.

**Rule:** one ad = one main promise.

Pick *one* angle:
- Pain-killer
- Before/after
- Myth-busting
- Speed
- Comparison
- Social proof

If you only do one thing from this tutorial, do this step.

## Step 1 — Get your “first win”: Nano Banana phone-selfie start frame (then write hooks)
This is where most beginners quit. They open a tool, see 50 options, and freeze.

So we flip it: **generate one realistic UGC start frame first**, so you immediately understand the “UGC look.”

### 1.1 The activation line (copy/paste)
Start your character prompt with:

`phone, front, camera, selfie`

Why it works: UGC is often filmed on a phone. This short phrase forces the model toward a handheld, creator-style composition.

### 1.2 Nano Banana character prompt template (fictional creator)
Use this template and change only the bracketed parts:

**Prompt**
```
phone, front, camera, selfie.
Realistic UGC-style selfie video frame of a [age range] [gender presentation] creator with natural skin texture, subtle imperfections, minimal makeup,
wearing [simple casual outfit], in a [simple real home setting like a white kitchen / bedroom / hallway] with natural window light.
Handheld phone perspective, slight lens distortion, shallow depth of field, candid expression, not posed, not studio lighting.
```

**Safety note:** use a fictional person or your own licensed likeness. Don’t try to recreate a real influencer.

### 1.3 Put your product in the creator’s hand (the “3×3 grid” trick)
In the reference workflow, the fastest way to get realistic hand-holding is:

1) Upload your product image.
2) Generate a **3×3 grid of different angles** of the product (so the model can match perspective).

**Prompt**
```
Create a 3x3 grid of different angles of this product. Clean, realistic product photography. Neutral background.
```

Then take:
- Reference image A: the creator selfie frame
- Reference image B: the product grid

**Prompt**
```
Place the product in the influencer's hand. Match the lighting of the product to the environment.
```

This gives you the most important UGC asset: **a believable start frame** that looks like a real person filming on a phone while holding your product.

### 1.4 Now write 10 hooks for the angle you chose
Don’t write a script yet. Write 10 hooks.

Use these hook formulas:
1) **Problem → blunt**: “If you’re still doing [old way], you’re wasting [time/money].”
2) **Confession**: “I didn’t want to admit this, but [pain] was my fault… until I did this.”
3) **Claim + proof preview**: “This [thing] improved [result]. I’ll show you the 3 steps.”
4) **Demo start**: “Watch this—here’s how I do [outcome] in under [time].”
5) **Do/Don’t**: “Don’t do [mistake]. Do this instead.”

Pick the best 2 hooks that are:
- Specific (time, niche, constraint)
- Proof-ready (you can show something within 5–10 seconds)

## Step 2 — Script + shot list + your first clip (keep models flexible)
At this point you have:
- one angle
- one start frame (creator holding product)
- two strong hooks

Now you write the script and convert it into a shot list.

### 2.1 The 6-block UGC script (copy/paste)
1) Hook (0–2s)
2) Context (2–5s)
3) Problem (5–8s)
4) Proof (8–18s)
5) Offer (18–25s)
6) CTA (last 2–4s)

Fill-in version:
**Hook:** “[your chosen hook]”
**Context:** “I’m [name/role], and I tried [thing] because [reason].”
**Problem:** “The issue with [old way] is [pain].”
**Proof:** “Here’s what changed: [demo], and the result was [proof].”
**Offer:** “This works because [one differentiator].”
**CTA:** “If you want [outcome], try [next step] today.”

### 2.2 Shot list (10 shots, 15–30s)
1) Face cam hook (tight crop)
2) Product close-up (1–2s)
3) “Here’s the problem” face cam
4) Demo clip #1
5) Caption-only emphasis
6) Demo clip #2
7) Proof clip (side-by-side / result / timeline)
8) Objection handling (one line)
9) Offer recap
10) CTA card

### 2.3 Generate your first short clip (optional, but motivating)
Use your start frame + your script to generate an 6–10 second “UGC-style” clip.

Keep the prompt simple:
```
UGC-style video of a creator filming on a phone, holding the product and speaking naturally.
[Paste your script here.]
```

Model choice detail (Kling vs Vidu vs Sora) is in the FAQ so you don’t get stuck here.

### 2.4 Build a “proof kit” (so you’re never stuck)
Most beginner UGC ads fail because they *say* the product is good but never *show* anything believable.

Create a simple proof kit you can reuse across ads:
- **1 screenshot proof**: a result, dashboard, before/after, or a clear deliverable (only if accurate and allowed)
- **1 demo proof**: a 3-step micro demo (tap/scroll/click)
- **1 objection clip**: “Here’s what I thought would be bad…”
- **1 comparison clip**: old way vs new way (side-by-side)
- **1 trust line**: a clear disclosure if you’re affiliated or incentivized

If you can’t show proof, change the angle to something you *can* prove (speed, workflow simplicity, fewer steps, fewer tools to manage).

## Step 3 — Edit with a “viral-style” recipe (clarity beats aesthetics)
- Cut every 0.7–1.5 seconds unless demo needs more time
- Captions: big, high contrast, 6–10 words per line
- Pattern interrupt every 3–5 seconds (zoom/b-roll/text/angle change)
- Keep one “hero idea” per ad

## Step 4 — Iterate with a testing grid (how beginners actually improve)
Use a small grid:
- 3 hooks (same angle)
- 2 edits (fast vs slower demo)
- 2 CTAs (Try it free vs Get the template)

That’s 12 variants. Improve with data, not taste.

## Step 5 — A beginner 7-day plan (Opus-style cadence)
If you’re starting from zero, don’t aim for “the perfect viral ad.” Aim for seven reps.

**Day 1 (Setup):** choose one angle + generate one Nano Banana selfie start frame + write 20 hooks.

**Day 2 (Proof kit):** assemble your proof kit (demo clip, comparison, one objection answer).

**Day 3 (Scripts):** write 5 scripts (6-block). Don’t edit yet.

**Day 4 (First cuts):** produce 3 ads quickly (same offer, different hook). Add captions.

**Day 5 (Variants):** for each ad, make 2 variants (caption style or opening visual).

**Day 6 (Test):** run the small testing grid (3 hooks × 2 edits × 2 CTAs if possible).

**Day 7 (Review):** keep winners, kill losers, rewrite 10 hooks based on what held attention.

The point is to build a repeatable system: angle → start frame → hooks → proof → CTA, repeated weekly.

## Common mistakes beginners make (and quick fixes)
1) **Hook is vague** → Add one constraint: time, cost, niche, or “without X.”
2) **Too many features** → Cut to one promise + one proof.
3) **Proof comes too late** → Move demo to within the first 8–10 seconds.
4) **Captions are unreadable** → Fewer words, bigger text, higher contrast.
5) **CTA is generic** → Match CTA to the angle (“Get the template” beats “Learn more”).

## FAQ (Models, workflow, and brand relationship)

### Why is Nano Banana Pro in the first two steps?
Because beginners need a fast “first win.” Nano Banana Pro is a strong image model for realistic humans and UGC-style composition. Once you have a believable start frame, hooks and scripts become easier because you can visualize what the ad is.

### Kling 2 vs Vidu vs Sora: which should beginners choose?
Pick based on what you’re generating:
- **Kling 2**: good for controllable short shots and “UGC b‑roll” style clips (simple movements, product moments, transitions).
- **Vidu**: often used for fast iterations and stylized short-form; choose it if you prioritize speed/iteration.
- **Sora**: choose it when you need higher realism or more complex scenes (and you’re willing to iterate more).
