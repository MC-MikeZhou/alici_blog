## Definitions (UGC / UGC Creator / UGC Ads / AI UGC Ads)

<!-- CITABLE_BLOCK: UGC definitions -->
**UGC (user-generated content)** is content created by users/customers (organic or incentivized), not by the brand. A **UGC creator** is a creator hired to produce *UGC-style deliverables* (usually without posting on their own account). **UGC Ads** are UGC-style videos used as paid ad creatives. **AI UGC Ads** are UGC-style ad creatives that are AI-assisted or AI-generated (script, scenes, voice, or variants). AI doesn’t change the strategy—it changes the production speed.
<!-- /CITABLE_BLOCK -->

### What UGC is (for creators)
- Short-form videos that look like real customer content (phone camera, natural lighting, simple edits)
- Deliverables brands can reuse on their channels and in ads (depending on rights)

### What UGC isn’t
- It’s not “influencer marketing by default” (you don’t need followers to start)
- It’s not permission to make unproven claims (proof matters more in 2026, not less)

## UGC vs Influencer Marketing: the real differences (AI UGC is *not* a third column)
Influencer marketing and UGC often use similar-looking content—but the business model is different:
- **UGC creators** are typically paid for **content deliverables**.
- **Influencers** are typically paid for **content + distribution** (their audience/reach).

Here’s a clean comparison:

| Factor | UGC (deliverables-only) | Influencer marketing (deliverables + reach) |
|---|---|---|
| What you sell | Video assets brands can use | Audience access + posts + (sometimes) usage rights |
| Typical pricing unit | Per video / bundle | Per post / campaign |
| Pricing examples (varies) | Billo reports market data clustering around **~$200 per short-form asset** (benchmark, not a promise). https://billo.app/blog/ugc-rates/ | Shopify cites an example: an influencer with 100k+ followers charging **$1,000** per static Instagram post and **$200** for an Instagram Story (rates vary). https://www.shopify.com/blog/influencer-marketing |
| Ownership/usage | Often negotiated usage rights for ads | Often limited rights unless you pay licensing |
| Best for | Conversion creative, creative testing, social proof | Awareness, launches, niche demographics |
| Scalability | Limited by creator capacity; improved by systems + templates | Limited by influencer capacity + campaign coordination |

**Where AI fits:** AI-assisted UGC can speed up scripting, storyboards, and variant generation—but it’s still UGC or influencer marketing depending on whether you’re selling deliverables or reach.

## Step 0 — Choose your lane (pick one to ship faster)
Beginners get stuck because they try to be “a creator for everyone.” Your goal is to look *reliable*, not “creative.”

Pick one lane for your first 30 days:
- Beauty / skincare (routine + result formats)
- Food / drinks (taste + convenience formats)
- Fitness / wellness (habits + progress formats)
- Home / gadgets (demo + before/after formats)
- Apps / SaaS (screen record + voiceover + tutorial formats)

**Rule:** choose a lane that makes filming easy. If you don’t own products in that category, start with **apps** or “generic” products you already have (water bottle, notebook, coffee maker).

### Your one-sentence positioning
Template:
> “I help **[type of brand]** create **[type of short-form UGC]** that emphasizes **[benefit]** in **[platform format]**.”

Example:
> “I help skincare brands create 15–30s ‘routine + result’ UGC videos optimized for TikTok and Reels.”

## Step 1 — Build a 5-video starter portfolio (no paid clients required)
Your portfolio is not a résumé. It’s proof you can ship content that matches what brands buy.

![Concept diagram: UGC Creator Flywheel — Portfolio → Pitch → Deliver → Iterate (repeatable system).](https://ct2.alici.ai/static/image/other/gen_images/ugc-creator-flywheel.png)

### The 5 videos (copy this list)
Make **5 videos** in the next 7 days:
1. **Hook + demo** (product): “I didn’t expect this to work, but…”
2. **Problem → solution** (product): “If you struggle with ___, try this.”
3. **Before/after** (product): show a visible change (same lighting)
4. **App walkthrough** (service/app): screen record + talking head
5. **Comparison** (service/app): option A vs B vs “what I use”

### Portfolio rules (brands notice these)
- **Audio clarity** beats camera quality.
- Keep the first 1–2 seconds extremely clear (one promise).
- Captions on every video (accessibility + retention).
- One consistent setup (same window light + background).

### Where to host it
Pick one:
- One-page site (best)
- Notion portfolio (fast)
- Google Drive folder (acceptable for first clients)

What to include on the portfolio page:
- 5 videos (embedded or linked)
- Your lane statement
- “What I deliver” list (deliverables)
- Contact link

## Step 2 — Create a starter offer menu (simple packages)
Pricing is a fear point. The fix is to price **packages**, not “time.”

Start with 3 packages and adjust after your first 3 projects:

### Package A — Single video
- 1× 15–30s UGC video
- 1 hook style (your choice)
- 1 round of revisions (script-level)

### Package B — 3-video bundle (best starter offer)
- 3× 15–30s UGC videos
- 3 different hooks
- 1 round of revisions per script

### Package C — 5-video bundle (iteration package)
- 5× 15–30s UGC videos
- 5 hook variants
- Deliverables pack (script + shot list + captions)

### Usage rights (beginner-safe line)
> Pricing above covers content creation. Usage rights (ads/whitelisting/extended licensing) are quoted separately.

This single line prevents the most common beginner mistake: accidentally granting unlimited usage for free.

> Not legal advice: usage rights language varies by platform and contract. If you’re unsure, review with counsel.

### Usage-rights checklist (copy/paste)
```text
USAGE RIGHTS CHECKLIST (not legal advice)

1) Where can the brand use the content?
- [ ] Organic only (brand socials, email, website)
- [ ] Paid ads (Meta, TikTok, YouTube, etc.)

2) How long?
- [ ] 30 days / 90 days / 6 months / 12 months / perpetual

3) What platforms + placements?
- [ ] TikTok / Reels / Shorts
- [ ] Feed / Stories / Spark Ads / Partnership ads / Whitelisting (if applicable)

4) Usage scope
- [ ] Single campaign only
- [ ] Any future campaigns
- [ ] Global / specific regions

5) Creator account usage
- [ ] Allowlisting / whitelisting requested? (yes/no)

6) Exclusivity
- [ ] Category exclusivity (yes/no) + duration

7) Deliverables + source files
- [ ] Raw footage included? (yes/no)
- [ ] Project files included? (yes/no)

8) Revisions
- [ ] Script revisions: ___ rounds
- [ ] Edit revisions: ___ rounds

9) Disclosure requirements
- [ ] Sponsored / paid partnership labeling required (yes/no)
```

## Step 4 — Use a brief + script + shot list (the “pro” system)
Brands pay for reliability. A simple system makes you reliable.

![Infographic: UGC Kit Templates — Brief + Script + Shot List (structure overview).](https://ct2.alici.ai/static/image/other/gen_images/ugc-brief-script-shotlist.png)

### UGC brief template
```text
UGC BRIEF — [Brand] — [Product] — [Date]

Goal:
- Primary action (click / signup / purchase / install):

Audience:
- Who is this for?
- Pain point / desire:

Offer:
- Core promise:
- Proof points (ONLY what’s true):

Constraints:
- Do-not-say claims:
- Must-include:
- Brand tone (3 adjectives):

Deliverables:
- # of videos:
- Length (15s/30s/45s):
- Format (TikTok/Reels/Shorts):
- Captions style:

Usage:
- Organic only or paid usage? (if paid: duration + platforms)
```

### 6-block UGC script template
```text
1) Hook (0–2s): one clear promise
2) Problem (2–5s): name the pain
3) Mechanism (5–10s): what it does / how it works (no fake claims)
4) Demo (10–20s): show it, don’t tell
5) Proof (20–25s): real detail (feature, routine, constraint)
6) CTA (last 2–3s): one action
```

### 10-shot list
```text
1) Talking head hook (front camera)
2) Product close-up (hands)
3) “Use it” demo (medium shot)
4) Texture/detail shot
5) Before/after (same lighting)
6) Benefit overlay (caption)
7) Social proof (only if provided)
8) FAQ answer clip (5s)
9) CTA clip (product + text)
10) Alternative hook variant
```

## Step 5 — Edit with a repeatable “short-form recipe”
You can keep editing simple and still look professional.

### A beginner edit recipe
- Cut every pause > 0.3s
- Captions on-screen (high contrast)
- 1–2 B‑roll inserts every 3–5 seconds
- Keep music low; voice is primary
- End with a clean CTA

### Caption style
- One line max
- Highlight key words (promise, time, constraint)

## Common mistakes beginners make (and quick fixes)
1. **Trying to look cinematic instead of clear** → choose clarity: lighting + audio + captions
2. **Five promises in one video** → one video = one main promise
3. **No brief** → use the brief template even for small jobs
4. **Unlimited usage rights for free** → separate “creation” vs “usage” in writing
5. **No iteration system** → sell bundles, not singles

Best for: SaaS, mobile apps, subscriptions

Best for: skincare, fitness, home, productivity

Best for: categories with alternatives (apps, tools, routines)  
3 script frameworks:
1. “If you want speed, pick A. If you want quality, pick B.”
2. “I tested both so you don’t have to.”

## AI UGC Ads in 2026: what they are (and a creator workflow that scales)
AI UGC Ads are **UGC-style ad creatives** that are AI-assisted or AI-generated (scripts, scenes, voice, or variants). The strategy is still UGC Ads—AI just changes how fast you can iterate.

### When AI-assisted UGC Ads make sense

## Glossary (plain English)
- **UGC**: user-generated content (customer/user-made content)
- **UGC creator**: hired creator producing UGC-style deliverables
- **UGC Ads**: UGC-style videos used as paid ad creatives

## FAQ

### Do I need followers to be a UGC creator?
No. UGC is about producing content brands can use. Some brands prefer a “real customer” vibe over influencer reach.

### What should I say about disclosures?
If content is sponsored, disclosures must be clear and not hidden. In the US, the FTC’s endorsement guidance emphasizes clear disclosure when there’s a “material connection.” Start with “Paid partnership” or “Sponsored.”  
FTC overview: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

### Should I use AI in my workflow?
AI can speed up script drafts, hook variants, storyboards, and multi-scene draft videos. Don’t use AI to invent performance claims, fake testimonials, or mislead viewers. In 2026, “proof-first” content beats “hype-first” content.

### What are “usage rights” in plain English?
It’s permission for the brand to use your content beyond initial delivery—especially for paid ads or extended licensing. Clarify scope (organic vs paid), duration, and platforms.
