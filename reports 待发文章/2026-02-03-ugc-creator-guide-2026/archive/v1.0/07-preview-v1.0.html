How to Become a UGC Creator in 2026: A Beginner Video Workflow (Step-by-Step)

A beginner-safe workflow with templates (portfolio, pitching, deliverables, rights)

9 min February 03, 2026

Key Takeaways (what you’ll build)

  • A 5‑video starter portfolio (3 product-style, 2 service/app-style)

  • A starter rate card (3 packages you can quote without panic)

  • A pitch kit (DM + email templates)

  • A delivery kit (brief + script + shot list + deliverables checklist)

  • A beginner-safe rights line so you don’t give away unlimited licensing for free

Quick Answer (40–60 words)

To become a UGC creator in 2026, pick one lane (category or app niche), create a 5‑video starter portfolio, set 3 starter packages, and pitch brands with a simple concept + script. Use a repeatable workflow—brief → script → shot list → edit recipe → delivery checklist—and clarify usage rights before you start.

Introduction (why UGC exists)

UGC works because it feels like a real customer, not a polished brand ad. Trust is the lever: Nielsen’s “Trust in Advertising” research reports 88% of global respondents trust recommendations from people they know more than any other channel. Nielsen (2021)

This guide is for UGC creator beginners focused on video UGC (Reels/TikTok/Shorts). It’s a workflow you can run in 7 days—no invented earnings claims, no “overnight success” myths.

Reference for broader UGC strategy context (not a rewrite): https://invideo.io/blog/ai-ugc-strategy-guide/

Step 0 — Choose your UGC lane (pick one)

Beginners get stuck because they try to be “a creator for everyone.”

Pick one lane for your first 30 days:
- Beauty / skincare
- Food / drinks
- Fitness / wellness
- Home / gadgets
- Apps / SaaS (screen-record + voiceover)

Rule: choose a lane that makes filming easy. If you don’t own products in that category, start with apps or “generic” products you already have (water bottle, notebook, coffee maker).

Your one-sentence positioning

Template:

“I help [type of brand] create [type of short-form UGC] that emphasizes [benefit] in [platform format].”

Example:

“I help skincare brands create 15–30s ‘routine + result’ UGC videos optimized for TikTok and Reels.”

Step 1 — Build a 5-video starter portfolio (no paid clients required)

Your portfolio is not a résumé. It’s proof you can ship content that matches what brands buy.

The 5 videos (copy this list)

Make 5 videos in the next 7 days:
1. Hook + demo (product): “I didn’t expect this to work, but…”
2. Problem → solution (product): “If you struggle with ___, try this.”
3. Before/after (product): show a visible change (same lighting)
4. App walkthrough (service/app): screen record + talking head
5. Comparison (service/app): option A vs B vs “what I use”

Portfolio rules (brands notice these)

  • Audio clarity beats camera quality.

  • Keep the first 1–2 seconds extremely clear (one promise).

  • Captions on every video (accessibility + retention).

  • One consistent setup (same window light + background).

Where to host it

Pick one:
- One-page site (best)
- Notion portfolio (fast)
- Google Drive folder (acceptable for first clients)

What to include on the portfolio page:
- 5 videos (embedded or linked)
- Your lane statement
- “What I deliver” list (deliverables)
- Contact link

Step 2 — Create a starter rate card (simple packages)

Pricing is a fear point. The fix is to price packages, not “time.”

Start with 3 packages and adjust after your first 3 projects:

Package A — Single video

  • 1× 15–30s UGC video

  • 1 hook style (your choice)

  • 1 round of revisions (script-level)

Package B — 3-video bundle (best starter offer)

  • 3× 15–30s UGC videos

  • 3 different hooks

  • 1 round of revisions per script

Package C — 5-video bundle (iteration package)

  • 5× 15–30s UGC videos

  • 5 hook variants

  • Deliverables pack (script + shot list + captions)

Usage rights (beginner-safe line)

Pricing above covers content creation. Usage rights (ads/whitelisting/extended licensing) are quoted separately.

This single line prevents the most common beginner mistake: accidentally granting unlimited usage for free.

Not legal advice: usage rights language varies by platform and contract. If you’re unsure, review with counsel.

Step 3 — Pitch brands (DM + email templates)

Your goal is to start conversations, not “close” in one message.

Who to pitch (beginner-friendly targets)

  • Brands already running TikTok/Reels ads (they understand creative testing)

  • Brands with strong products but weak creator content

  • Apps that need walkthrough videos

Pitch DM template (short)

Hey [name] — I’m a UGC creator focused on [lane]. I drafted a quick 15–30s concept for [brand] (hook + demo). If you’re open, I can send the concept + a rough script. Where should I send it?

Pitch email template (copy/paste)

Subject: UGC concept for [Brand] (15–30s)

Hi [Name],

Portfolio: [link]

Best,

Step 4 — Use a brief + script + shot list (the “pro” system)

Brands pay for reliability. A simple system makes you reliable.

UGC brief template

Goal:
- Primary action (click / signup / purchase / install):

Audience:
- Who is this for?
- Pain point / desire:

Offer:
- Core promise:
- Proof points (ONLY what’s true):

Constraints:
- Do-not-say claims:
- Must-include:
- Brand tone (3 adjectives):

Deliverables:
- # of videos:
- Length (15s/30s/45s):
- Format (TikTok/Reels/Shorts):
- Captions style:

Usage:
- Organic only or paid usage? (if paid: duration + platforms)

6-block UGC script template

1) Hook (0–2s): one clear promise
2) Problem (2–5s): name the pain
3) Mechanism (5–10s): what it does / how it works (no fake claims)
4) Demo (10–20s): show it, don’t tell
5) Proof (20–25s): real detail (feature, routine, constraint)
6) CTA (last 2–3s): one action

10-shot list

1) Talking head hook (front camera)
2) Product close-up (hands)
3) “Use it” demo (medium shot)
4) Texture/detail shot
5) Before/after (same lighting)
6) Benefit overlay (caption)
7) Social proof (only if provided)
8) FAQ answer clip (5s)
9) CTA clip (product + text)
10) Alternative hook variant

Step 5 — Edit with a repeatable “short-form recipe”

You can keep editing simple and still look professional.

A beginner edit recipe

  • Cut every pause > 0.3s

  • Captions on-screen (high contrast)

  • 1–2 B‑roll inserts every 3–5 seconds

  • Keep music low; voice is primary

  • End with a clean CTA

Caption style

  • One line max

  • Highlight key words (promise, time, constraint)

Step 6 — Deliver like a pro (checklist)

Deliverables are where beginners lose trust: files missing, naming inconsistent, no versions.

Deliverables checklist

DELIVERABLES — [Brand] — [Date]

Video files:
- [ ] 1x final MP4 (1080x1920)
- [ ] 1x captioned MP4 (burned-in)
- [ ] 1x clean MP4 (no captions)

Project notes:
- [ ] Hook used (angle)
- [ ] CTA used
- [ ] Any claims made (confirm approved)

Source files (optional):
- [ ] captions file (.srt)
- [ ] thumbnail frame(s)

Licensing:
- [ ] usage rights confirmed (organic vs paid, duration, platforms)

Common mistakes beginners make (and quick fixes)

  1. Trying to look cinematic instead of clear → choose clarity: lighting + audio + captions

  2. Five promises in one video → one video = one main promise

  3. No brief → use the brief template even for small jobs

  4. Unlimited usage rights for free → separate “creation” vs “usage” in writing

  5. No iteration system → sell bundles, not singles

FAQ

Do I need followers to be a UGC creator?

No. UGC is about producing content brands can use. Some brands prefer a “real customer” vibe over influencer reach.

What should I say about disclosures?

If content is sponsored, disclosures must be clear and not hidden. In the US, the FTC’s endorsement guidance emphasizes clear disclosure when there’s a “material connection.” Start with “Paid partnership” or “Sponsored.”
FTC overview: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews

Should I use AI in my workflow?

AI can speed up script drafts, hook variants, and storyboards. Don’t use AI to invent performance claims, fake testimonials, or mislead viewers.

What are “usage rights” in plain English?

It’s permission for the brand to use your content beyond initial delivery—especially for paid ads or extended licensing. Clarify scope (organic vs paid), duration, and platforms.

Methodology (E‑E‑A‑T, in plain English)

  • Experience: designed to be run by beginners in 7 days (templates + checklists).

  • Expertise: emphasis on deliverables and repeatable systems.

  • Authoritativeness: citations for policy and “why it works” claims.

  • Trust: no fabricated earnings/performance claims; clear disclosures.
