## What competitors cluster around (intent patterns)
From the corpus, the dominant user intents (and what people actually search/read for) cluster into:
1) **Definitions**: what UGC is, what UGC creators do, UGC vs influencer marketing  
2) **Start path**: become a UGC creator, portfolio basics, what to film first  
3) **Pricing**: rates, packages, what affects price  
4) **Rights**: usage rights, allowlisting/whitelisting, “paid vs organic” terms  
5) **Get clients**: outreach/pitching, platforms/marketplaces, “brands looking for creators”  
6) **UGC Ads**: why they work, brief + iteration, creative testing systems  
7) **Compliance**: FTC disclosure, platform branded content policies  
8) **AI / synthetic**: AI-assisted UGC creation, speed/scale tradeoffs, authenticity guardrails

## Competitor strengths (what we keep)
- “Complete guide” structure is proven (InVideo, Shopify, Later): **definitions → examples → steps → FAQs**.
- Billo’s ecosystem covers the *real* beginner blockers well: **creator workflow, rates, usage rights, UGC ads**.
- HeyGen captures the emerging “AI UGC ads” curiosity: people want speed + scalability.

## Major gaps (what we exploit in v2.0)
1) **Money hook is often vague** (“UGC works”) instead of *budget + demand* backed by sources.  
2) Many guides are **brand-led** and skip the creator’s deliverable system (brief → script → shot list → delivery).  
3) Rights content exists, but the *tutorial* rarely includes **creator-safe checklists + plain-English lines** that prevent bad licensing deals.  
4) AI sections often drift into hype or tool lists; they rarely define **AI UGC Ads boundaries** and **compliance implications** clearly.  
5) Few guides present niche selection as **deliverable shapes** (demo / walkthrough / before-after / comparison) that are easy for beginners to execute.
