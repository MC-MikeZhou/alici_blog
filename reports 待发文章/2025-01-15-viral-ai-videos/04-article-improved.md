## Step 2: Understand the Viral Prompt Formula

The difference between forgettable AI videos and viral hits lies in prompt structure. Successful prompts follow a consistent formula.

**The Viral Prompt Structure:**

```
[Familiar Format] + [Unexpected Subject/Twist] + [Specific Details] + [Tone/Style]
```

**Element 1: Familiar Format**
Use a visual format viewers instantly recognize: bodycam footage, movie scene, TV show style, documentary, security camera, news broadcast. Recognition creates comfort; the viewer knows what to expect visually.

**Element 2: Unexpected Twist**
Introduce something that doesn't belong: a penguin in a business meeting, Darth Vader as a flight attendant, a dog giving a TED talk. The cognitive dissonance between format and subject creates shareability.

**Element 3: Specific Details**
Vague prompts produce generic results. Specify: camera angles, lighting conditions, character expressions, environmental details, movement patterns.

**Element 4: Tone/Style**
Define the emotional register: "shot on iPhone," "cinematic 4K," "VHS aesthetic," "professional documentary style." This prevents the AI from guessing wrong.

**Example Prompt:**
```
Bodycam footage of a police officer pulling over a penguin driving
a tiny car on the highway. The penguin nervously hands over its
license. Shot in realistic documentary style, slight camera shake,
afternoon lighting, the officer maintains professional composure.
```

## Step 3: Write Your First Viral Prompt

Now apply the formula to create your own prompt. Follow this step-by-step process.

**Start with trending formats.** Current high-performing formats include:
- Bodycam comedy scenarios
- Movie scene mashups (Breaking Bad + SpongeBob)
- "Day in the life" of unusual subjects
- Retro aesthetic recreations (80s commercial, VHS home video)
- Impossible personal situations

**Add your twist.** Ask yourself: "What would make someone stop scrolling?" The best twists create visual absurdity while maintaining internal logic. A cat attending a job interview is absurd but visually coherent.

**Layer in specifics.** Transform vague ideas into detailed scenes:

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| "A dog at work" | "Golden retriever in a suit giving a quarterly earnings presentation, PowerPoint slides visible, conference room with fluorescent lighting" |
| "Funny alien video" | "Security camera footage of an alien trying to use a self-checkout machine at 2 AM, frustrated expressions, flickering store lights" |

**Specify technical elements.** Add camera direction, lighting, and style:
- Camera: "handheld," "static shot," "slow zoom," "tracking shot"
- Lighting: "golden hour," "harsh fluorescent," "dramatic shadows"
- Style: "documentary," "cinematic," "found footage," "commercial"

**What you should see**: A complete prompt of 40-80 words that includes format, subject, specific details, and style direction.

## Step 4: Generate and Iterate

With your prompt ready, it's time to generate. This step requires patience and iteration.

**First generation is rarely final.** Expect to regenerate 3-5 times before getting usable content. Each generation teaches you how the model interprets your language.

**Adjust based on results:**
- If the scene is too static → Add movement descriptions ("walking," "gesturing," "turning")
- If the mood is wrong → Strengthen tone descriptors ("tense," "comedic," "awkward silence")
- If details are missing → Be more explicit about what you need to see

**Use image inputs for consistency.** Most tools accept reference images. Upload a character design, set photo, or style reference to anchor the generation. This is especially useful for maintaining character appearance across multiple clips. ([OpenAI Sora Prompting Guide](https://cookbook.openai.com/examples/sora/sora2_prompting_guide))

**Shorter clips are more reliable.** A 5-second clip will have fewer errors than a 20-second clip. For complex scenes, generate multiple short clips and edit them together.

**Save successful prompts.** When a generation works, document the exact prompt. Build a personal library of proven prompts that you can modify for future content.

**What you should see**: After 3-5 iterations, you should have at least one clip that captures your intended scene with acceptable quality.

## Step 6: Edit for Maximum Impact

Raw AI output needs refinement. Simple edits dramatically improve performance.

**Essential edits:**
1. **Trim the fat** - Remove slow starts. The interesting part should begin immediately.
2. **Add text overlays** - Context text ("POV: you're late to work") increases comprehension
3. **Include captions** - 85% of social video is watched without sound ([Verizon Media Study](https://www.verizon.com/about/news/verizon-media-says-69-percent-consumers-watching-video-sound-off))

**The 3-second rule:** If viewers haven't been hooked by second 3, they're gone. Front-load your most interesting visual.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Prompts That Are Too Vague

**Problem**: Generic prompts like "funny video of a cat" produce generic results that blend into the content ocean.

**Why it happens**: Beginners underestimate how literally AI interprets instructions.

**Solution**: Always include format, specific details, and style. "Funny cat video" becomes "Security camera footage of a cat knocking items off a desk one by one, maintaining eye contact with the camera, office setting, 3 AM timestamp visible."

### 2. Ignoring Platform Requirements

**Problem**: Creating landscape videos for TikTok or 2-minute clips for Shorts.

**Why it happens**: Treating AI video as a single output rather than platform-specific content.

**Solution**: Define your target platform before generating. Generate in the correct aspect ratio from the start.

### 3. Over-Complicating Scenes

**Problem**: Requesting multiple characters, complex interactions, and scene changes in a single prompt.

**Why it happens**: Trying to create complete narratives in one generation.

**Solution**: Break complex ideas into single-shot prompts. Generate multiple simple clips and edit them together.

### 4. Skipping the Iteration Process

**Problem**: Giving up after one or two generations that don't match expectations.

**Why it happens**: Unrealistic expectations about AI consistency.

**Solution**: Budget for 5-10 generations per final clip. Treat generation as a collaborative process, not a one-shot solution.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long should AI videos be for TikTok?

The optimal length for TikTok AI videos is 7-15 seconds. Videos under 60 seconds are eligible for the For You Page algorithm boost. Shorter videos have higher completion rates, which signals quality to the algorithm. However, ensure your content delivers a complete idea—a rushed 5-second clip with no payoff performs worse than a satisfying 15-second story.

### Which AI video generator is best for beginners?

Kling 2.0 is the most beginner-friendly AI video generator. It offers open access without waitlists, generates quickly, and produces consistent results with simple prompts. Once comfortable with prompt writing, graduate to Sora 2 for higher quality output. Runway Gen-4 suits creators who want built-in editing tools.

### How much does it cost to create AI videos?

Costs vary by platform. Sora 2 requires ChatGPT Plus ($20/month) for basic access or ChatGPT Pro ($200/month) for extended features. Kling 2.0 offers free tiers with paid upgrades. ### Can AI-generated videos be monetized?

Yes, AI-generated videos can be monetized on most platforms. YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram allow AI content in monetization programs. However, you must have commercial rights to the AI tool's output—check each platform's terms. Disclose AI usage where required. Original creative direction (your prompts and editing) establishes your ownership claim.

### How do I make AI videos look more realistic?

Improve realism by: 1) Using specific lighting descriptions ("golden hour," "overcast daylight"), 2) Adding camera imperfections ("slight handheld shake," "lens flare"), 3) Specifying realistic environments with texture details, 4) Keeping scenes simple—fewer elements means fewer AI errors, 5) Using reference images to anchor style and character appearance.
