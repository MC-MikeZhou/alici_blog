Most AI video tools force you to describe movement in words. "Make her dance gracefully" becomes a guessing game between you and the model. Motion Control flips this: you show the AI what you want by uploading a reference video. Combined with Nano Banana Pro's image quality, you get precise control over both appearance and movement.

The counterintuitive insight? Success isn't about finding the perfect action clip — it's about choosing the right mode.

TL;DR: Nano Banana Pro generates high-quality character images while Kling 2.6 Motion Control adds movement from reference videos. Match Image mode (10s max) preserves character details; Match Video mode (30s max) copies exact skeletal motion. You can generate your own reference videos within 11 Labs.

Direct Answer

Nano Banana Pro creates high-quality character images inside 11 Labs. Kling 2.6 Motion Control then transfers movements from any reference video onto that image. The key decision is choosing between Match Image mode (preserves character details, max 10 seconds) or Match Video mode (copies exact motion, max 30 seconds). Text prompts are optional — they control background and lighting, not the actual movement.

Key Takeaways

Match Image (10s) — prioritizes character consistency. Textures and facial features stay true to your original image, but the AI may invent camera movements.
Match Video (30s) — prioritizes motion accuracy. Exact skeletal movement is copied, but character proportions may stretch to fit.
Text prompts are optional — the reference video dictates all action; prompts only affect background and lighting.
Generate reference videos internally — create a neutral-pose character in Nano Banana Pro, then animate it with Kling 2.6 to build your own motion library.

Deep Dive: Four Dimensions

1. The Complete Workflow

Start by creating your character in Nano Banana Pro at 2K resolution. Upload a reference photo, describe the scene (outfit, setting, pose), and generate multiple options. Pick your favorite, then switch to video mode. Select Kling 2.6 Motion Control, drag your image in, upload a motion reference video, and generate.

2. Match Image vs Match Video — How to Choose

Factor Match Image Match Video
Max Duration 10 seconds 30 seconds
Priority Character appearance Motion accuracy
Risk May invent camera moves Character may stretch
Best For Short clips, identity-critical Longer sequences, precise choreography

Use Match Image when the character's look matters more than perfect motion replication. Use Match Video when you need exact movements and can tolerate minor character distortion.

3. Prompt Strategy

Here's what surprises most users: prompts don't control movement. When you add text to Motion Control, it affects scene elements — background details, lighting atmosphere, environmental context. The reference video handles all motion. Many creators leave prompts blank and see no difference in output quality.

4. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Restrictive starting poses: If your character image has arms crossed or an unusual stance, the AI struggles to map different movements onto it. Start with a neutral pose — arms by sides, centered framing.

No reference video? Create one. Generate a simple figure in Nano Banana Pro with neutral pose, switch to Kling 2.6, and prompt movements like "do a little tango dance" or "slowly walk backwards." Use these generated videos as motion references for your actual character images.

Why It Works

Motion Control separates two problems that text-to-video muddles together: what something looks like and how it moves. Nano Banana Pro handles appearance with high-fidelity image generation. The reference video handles motion through direct visual specification. This division of labor means less guessing, fewer wasted generations, and results that match your creative intent.

Mini FAQ

Do I need a text prompt for Motion Control?

No. Prompts are optional and only affect background/lighting. The reference video controls all movement. Many users leave prompts blank.

Can I create my own reference videos without filming?

Yes. Generate a neutral-pose character in Nano Banana Pro, then use Kling 2.6 (not Motion Control) to animate it with a text prompt describing the movement you want. Save that video as your motion reference.

Which mode should I use if I'm unsure?

Start with Match Image for shorter clips where character consistency matters. Switch to Match Video if you need longer duration or precise motion matching and can accept some character distortion.
