### Step 2: Upload Your Photo

Not all photos animate well. Choose images with:

- **Clear subject**: A person, animal, or object that can logically move
- **Good resolution**: Minimum 1024x1024 pixels (ideally 2048x2048)
- **Natural composition**: Avoid heavily edited or collaged images

## Understanding Animation Presets

Animation presets are pre-configured settings that optimize AI behavior for specific types of content. Instead of writing complex prompts, you select a preset that matches your photo type, and the AI handles the details.

### The 4 Core Presets

Most modern photo-to-video tools offer variations of these four preset types:

| Preset | Best For | Motion Type | Example Output |
|--------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| **Portrait Smile** | Headshots, selfies, ID photos | Gentle blinking, subtle smile, slight head turn | LinkedIn photo comes alive with natural expression |
| **Group Shot** | Team photos, family pictures | Multiple people waving, smiling, slight movement | Wedding photo where everyone waves at camera |
| **Product** | E-commerce, product photography | 360-degree rotation, lighting glides across surface | Sneaker rotating like a mini commercial |
| **Universal** | Landscapes, artwork, anything else | Camera drift, depth parallax, atmospheric motion | Mountain scene with drifting clouds and depth |

### When to Use Each Preset

**Portrait Smile** works best when:
- Your photo shows a single person's face clearly
- You want subtle, natural-looking motion
- The subject's expression can logically shift to a smile
- You need professional-looking results for social media or business

**Group Shot** works best when:
- Your photo contains 2-6 people
- Everyone's face is visible and not overlapping
- You want coordinated movement (waving, nodding)
- The setting suggests social interaction

**Product** works best when:
- Your photo shows a single product against a clean background
- You want commercial-quality rotating animation
- The product has interesting textures or details
- You are creating e-commerce or marketing content

**Universal** works best when:
- Your photo is a landscape, architectural shot, or artwork
- No specific preset matches your content
- You want cinematic camera movement
- Adding atmospheric elements (clouds, leaves, water) would enhance the scene

### Preset vs Custom Prompt: When to Choose

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| First-time user | Start with presets |
| Specific vision for the animation | Use custom prompt |
| Quick social media content | Presets are faster |
| Portfolio or professional work | Custom prompts for control |
| Unusual photo type | Custom prompt required |

The best way to learn photo-to-video AI is through practical examples. Here are six common scenarios with complete prompts you can adapt for your own photos.

**Photo type**: Front-facing selfie or professional headshot

**Recommended method**: Portrait Smile preset OR custom prompt

**Complete prompt**:

## Prompt Mastery: From Basic to Cinematic

Understanding prompt structure separates amateur animations from professional-quality output. This section covers the complete framework for writing effective photo-to-video prompts.

### The 4-Part Prompt Formula

<!-- CITABLE_BLOCK: 4-Part Prompt Formula -->
The most effective photo-to-video prompts follow a consistent structure: Camera Direction + Subject Action + Scene Detail + Mood/Tone. This formula works across all major AI video models because it addresses the four key decisions the AI must make when generating motion.
<!-- /CITABLE_BLOCK -->

**Formula breakdown**:

```
[Camera direction] + [Subject action] + [Scene detail] + [Mood/tone]
```

**Example application**:

| Component | Example | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Camera direction | "Slow push-in camera" | Tells AI how the viewpoint moves |
| Subject action | "The woman turns her head gently and smiles warmly" | Defines what the main subject does |
| Scene detail | "Soft afternoon light filters through the window" | Adds environmental context |
| Mood/tone | "Intimate, serene mood" | Guides the overall feel |

**Complete prompt using formula**:
> "Slow push-in camera. The woman turns her head gently and smiles warmly. Soft afternoon light filters through the window. Intimate, serene mood."

### Motion Intensity Levels

Not all animations should have the same amount of movement. Control intensity to match your content:

| Level | Keywords to Use | Best For | Example |
|-------|-----------------|----------|---------|
| **Subtle** | "gentle," "subtle," "slight," "minimal" | Portraits, professional content | "Subtle camera drift, gentle blink" |
| **Moderate** | "smooth," "flowing," "gradual," "steady" | Products, lifestyle photos | "Smooth camera track, flowing motion" |
| **Dynamic** | "dramatic," "sweeping," "rapid," "energetic" | Landscapes, action shots | "Dynamic sweeping motion, rapid camera movement" |

**Intensity guidelines by content type**:

| Content Type | Recommended Intensity | Why |
|--------------|----------------------|-----|
| Portraits | Subtle | Faces distort with too much motion |
| Products | Moderate | Need visible movement for commercial appeal |
| Landscapes | Moderate to Dynamic | Large scenes can handle more motion |
| Artwork | Subtle | Respect the original artistic intent |
| Pets/Animals | Moderate | Natural animal movements are quick but contained |

### Cinematic Camera Terminology

Use these terms for predictable results across all AI video models:

| Term | Effect | Best For |
|------|--------|----------|
| `push-in` | Camera moves toward subject | Dramatic reveals, emotional moments |
| `pull-out` | Camera moves away from subject | Establishing context, endings |
| `dolly left/right` | Camera slides horizontally | Following action, revealing scenes |
| `pan left/right` | Camera rotates on axis | Surveying wide scenes |
| `tilt up/down` | Camera rotates vertically | Tall subjects, reveals |
| `crane up/down` | Camera rises or lowers | Epic establishing shots |
| `orbit` | Camera circles around subject | Product showcases, 360 views |
| `static` | No camera movement | Focus on subject motion only |
| `handheld` | Subtle organic shake | Documentary feel, intimacy |

### Troubleshooting Table: Common Issues and Fixes

| Issue | Likely Cause | Solution |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| **Distorted faces** | Motion intensity too high | Use Portrait preset, add "subtle" to prompt |
| **Frozen background** | No environmental motion specified | Add "ambient motion" or specific background actions |
| **Unnatural movement** | Vague motion descriptions | Use cinematic terms (dolly, pan) instead of "moves" |
| **Flickering/artifacts** | Low resolution source image | Use images 2048px or larger |
| **Wrong direction** | Ambiguous spatial terms | Use "camera left/right" not just "left/right" |
| **Too fast/slow** | No timing specified | Add "over X seconds" or "gradually" |
| **Lost details** | AI over-processing | Add "preserve original details" to prompt |
| **Inconsistent subject** | Multiple focal points | Specify "main subject" explicitly |

### Advanced: Multi-Shot Narratives

For longer or more complex animations, structure your prompt with shot timing:

**Multi-shot prompt example**:
> "[0-3s] Camera slowly pushes in on the woman's face. She blinks gently. [3-5s] Her expression shifts from contemplation to a warm smile. [5-8s] Camera continues push-in as golden light intensifies on her features."

**Key principles for multi-shot prompts**:
- Use time brackets: `[0-3s]`, `[3-6s]`
- Each segment gets one primary action
- Maintain consistent subject descriptions across segments
- End with a conclusive moment (smile, reveal, etc.)

## Best Practices and Technical Tips

### Photo Selection Criteria

The source image determines 60% of your output quality. Follow these standards:

**Resolution requirements**:
- Minimum: 1024 x 1024 pixels
- Recommended: 2048 x 2048 pixels
- Maximum benefit: 4096 x 4096 pixels (diminishing returns above this)

**Composition guidelines**:
- Subject should occupy 30-70% of frame
- Leave space in the direction of intended motion
- Avoid edge-to-edge subjects

**Technical quality**:
- Sharp focus on main subject
- Proper exposure (avoid blown highlights or crushed shadows)
- Minimal noise or compression artifacts

### How Photo-to-Video AI Works

<!-- CITABLE_BLOCK: First Frame Principle -->
Photo-to-video AI operates on the "First Frame Principle": your uploaded image becomes the first frame of the video, and the AI model predicts subsequent frames based on learned patterns from millions of training videos. This differs fundamentally from text-to-video, where the AI must imagine the entire scene from scratch.
<!-- /CITABLE_BLOCK -->

Modern image-to-video models like Wan 2.1, Kling, and Runway use diffusion techniques combined with motion prediction. The AI:

1. **Analyzes your image** - Identifies subjects, depth, lighting, and scene context
2. **Predicts motion trajectories** - Determines how elements should move naturally
3. **Generates intermediate frames** - Creates smooth transitions between predicted positions
4. **Renders final output** - Produces a 4-10 second video clip at 24fps

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I animate any photo to video with AI?

Most photos can be animated, but results vary significantly based on image quality and content. Photos with clear subjects, good resolution (1024px+), and natural compositions work best. Abstract art, heavily filtered images, or photos with multiple overlapping subjects often produce unpredictable results. Use the Universal preset for unusual content types.

### Which preset should I use for my photo?

Match your photo type to the preset: Portrait Smile for headshots and selfies, Group Shot for 2-6 people, Product for e-commerce items, and Universal for landscapes, artwork, or anything else. When in doubt, start with Universal and refine with a custom prompt if needed.

### How do I avoid distorted faces in animations?

Face distortion typically occurs when motion intensity is too high. Use the Portrait Smile preset, which is optimized for facial expressions. In custom prompts, add "subtle" or "gentle" before any facial movements, and avoid asking for dramatic head rotations or exaggerated expressions. Ensure your source image is at least 2048px resolution.

### How long are AI-generated videos from photos?

Most tools generate 3-10 second clips from a single image. Wan 2.1 typically produces 4-8 seconds at 24fps. WaveSpeedAI supports up to 15-second clips. Longer videos require either multiple generations stitched together or premium tools with extended duration features.

### Do I need special hardware to animate photos with AI?
