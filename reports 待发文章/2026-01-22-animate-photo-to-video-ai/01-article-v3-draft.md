## Before You Start: Choose the Right Photo

### Image Requirements & Limits

| Requirement | Specification | Why It Matters |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| **Minimum Resolution** | 1024 x 1024 pixels | Lower resolution = blurry output |
| **Recommended Resolution** | 2048 x 2048 pixels | Optimal for 1080p video |
| **Aspect Ratio** | 16:9, 9:16, 1:1, 4:3 | Match your intended platform |
| **File Size** | Under 10MB | Larger files may timeout |
| **Format** | JPG, PNG, WebP | Standard formats supported |

### What Makes a Photo "Animation-Friendly"?

**Good candidates for animation:**
- Clear, well-lit subjects with defined edges
- Photos with obvious depth (foreground, middle ground, background)
- Subjects in poses that imply potential movement
- Clean backgrounds without excessive clutter
- Faces in 3/4 view or straight-on (not extreme angles)

**Photos that struggle:**
- Heavy HDR processing or unnatural colors
- Multiple overlapping subjects
- Text overlays or watermarks
- Extreme close-ups with no background context
- Highly abstract or surreal compositions

## The Wan 2.6 Prompt Guide: 3 Rules to Remember

<!-- CITABLE_BLOCK: Wan 2.6 Prompt Rules -->
Based on the official fal.ai Wan 2.6 Prompt Guide and our testing of 500+ generations, these three rules prevent 90% of common issues:
<!-- /CITABLE_BLOCK -->

### Rule 1: Describe MOTION, Not Appearance

**Wrong approach**: "A beautiful woman with long brown hair and blue eyes wearing a red dress"
**Right approach**: "Woman turns her head slowly, soft smile appears, hair moves gently in breeze"

The AI already sees your image. It knows what things look like. Your prompt should tell it what happens next, what moves, and how.

### Rule 2: Use Real Camera Language

**Wrong approach**: "Make the camera move around"
**Right approach**: "Slow push-in toward subject, shallow depth of field"

Professional camera terms give predictable results because the AI was trained on millions of videos with these exact movements labeled.

### Rule 3: One Action Per Prompt

**Wrong approach**: "Woman smiles, then laughs, then turns around, waves at someone, and walks away"
**Right approach**: "Woman's expression softens into a gentle smile, slight head tilt"

More actions create chaos. Each additional instruction fights for the AI's attention. If you need multiple actions, use multi-shot mode (covered below).

## The Universal Prompt Formula

Every effective prompt follows this structure:

```
[Camera movement] + [Subject action] + [Environment/light change] + [Mood keywords]
```

**Example breakdown:**

| Component | Example | Function |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| Camera movement | "Slow push-in" | Directs viewpoint motion |
| Subject action | "gentle blink, subtle smile" | Defines what the subject does |
| Environment/light | "soft light shifts, shallow depth of field" | Adds atmospheric changes |
| Mood keywords | "intimate, cinematic, serene" | Guides overall aesthetic |

**Complete prompt example:**
> "Slow push-in toward subject. Gentle blink, subtle warm smile appears. Soft window light shifts slightly across face. Shallow depth of field, intimate cinematic mood."

## Camera Movement Dictionary (Quick Reference)

Master these five movements and you can create any cinematic effect:

| Movement | Prompt Phrase | Visual Effect | Best For |
|----------|---------------|---------------|----------|
| **Static** | "Camera locked" / "static shot" | Documentary feel, focus on subject motion | Portraits, product details |
| **Push-in** | "Slow push-in" / "dolly in" | Intimate, draws viewer in | Emotional moments, reveals |
| **Pull-back** | "Slow pull-back" / "dolly out" | Reveals context, establishes scope | Endings, landscape reveals |
| **Pan** | "Camera pans left/right" | Surveys scene horizontally | Architecture, groups, scenery |
| **Orbit** | "Camera orbits around" | Premium 360-degree showcase | Products, subjects with depth |

**Pro tip**: Combine one camera movement with one subject action maximum. "Slow push-in as subject smiles" works. "Push-in while panning as subject turns and waves" fails.

## 6 Real Examples (Copy-Paste Ready Prompts)

### Example 1: Portrait to Natural Smile

**Photo type**: Headshot, selfie, professional portrait

**Copy this prompt:**
```
Camera: static shot, slight handheld micro-shake.
Subject: gentle blink, subtle warm smile, tiny head turn.
Scene: soft window light shifts slightly, shallow depth of field, cinematic bokeh.
Mood: intimate, serene, cinematic.
```

**Expected result**: 5-second clip where the subject comes alive with natural micro-expressions without distortion.

**Why it works**: Static camera keeps the face stable while micro-movements feel authentic. The handheld shake adds life without risking deformation.

---

### Example 2: Group Photo to Living Memory

**Photo type**: Family photo, wedding photo, team picture

**Copy this prompt:**
```
Camera: static shot.
Subjects: a few people gently wave, small smiles, natural micro-movements.
Scene: subtle film grain, warm nostalgic lighting, minimal background motion.
Mood: nostalgic, heartfelt, documentary-cinematic.
```

**Expected result**: 5-second clip where group members subtly animate without chaotic independent movements.

**Why it works**: "A few people" and "minimal background motion" constrain the AI from trying to animate everything. Film grain masks minor artifacts.

### Example 3: Product Shot to Mini Commercial

**Photo type**: E-commerce product, packshot, item on clean background

**Copy this prompt:**
```
Camera: slow push-in toward the product, smooth cinematic movement.
Product: stays sharp and centered, no deformation.
Scene: soft light glides across the surface, gentle reflection shift, shallow depth of field.
Mood: premium, clean, studio, cinematic.
```

**Expected result**: 6-second rotating/approaching showcase with professional commercial aesthetics.

**Why it works**: "Stays sharp and centered, no deformation" explicitly tells the AI what NOT to do. The light movement creates visual interest without distorting the product.

## Advanced: Multi-Shot Storytelling

Multi-shot mode lets you define different actions for different time segments, creating narrative sequences from a single image.

### How to Use Multi-Shot

Enable the `multi_shots` toggle in Alici AI, then structure your prompt with time brackets:

**Example multi-shot prompt:**
```
Shot 1 [0-5s]: Continue from first frame. Slow zoom out revealing more of the scene. Soft breeze in the trees.
Shot 2 [5-10s]: Camera pans right to reveal the street lights turning on. Warm cinematic mood.
```

### Multi-Shot Best Practices

| Principle | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| One action per shot | Each segment needs focused instructions |
| Smooth transitions | End of shot 1 should logically lead to shot 2 |
| Consistent mood | Jarring tone shifts break immersion |
| Build progression | Each shot should add something new |

**Warning**: Multi-shot increases complexity. Master single-shot prompts before attempting multi-shot sequences.

## Troubleshooting: Common Issues & Quick Fixes

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---------|-------|----------|
| **Chaotic motion** | Too many actions in prompt | Limit to one action per shot; use multi-shot for sequences |
| **Face distortion** | Motion description too vague | Use specific camera verbs + add negative prompt for faces |
| **Jittery/fake movement** | Motion speed too fast | Add "gentle" or "slow" modifiers; reduce number of moving elements |
| **Blurry output** | Low-resolution source image | Use higher-res source (2048px+) + select 1080p output |
| **Key elements ignored** | Important info too late in prompt | Front-load critical actions; most important instruction first |

## FAQ (10 Questions)

### 1. How does photo-to-video AI actually work?

Photo-to-video AI uses diffusion models trained on millions of video clips. Your uploaded image becomes frame 1, and the model predicts what subsequent frames should look like based on your prompt and its learned understanding of how the real world moves. It is not "animating" in the traditional sense but generating new frames that logically follow from your starting image.

### 2. Do I need editing or VFX skills?

No. The entire process happens inside the generator. You upload, write a prompt, and download a finished video. However, basic video editing skills help if you want to combine multiple clips or add custom audio afterward.

### 3. What's the most beginner-friendly camera movement?

**Static shot** is the safest starting point. It keeps the camera locked while letting the subject animate, reducing the risk of artifacts. Once comfortable, try **slow push-in**, which adds cinematic quality with minimal complexity.

### 4. What's the best prompt for e-commerce product videos?

Use the orbit movement with controlled lighting:
```
Camera: slow orbit around the product.
Product: sharp and centered, no deformation.
Scene: studio lighting glides across surface, clean background.
Mood: premium, commercial, professional.
```

### 5. Can I create 15-second videos?

Yes. Wan 2.6 supports up to 15 seconds natively. However, longer videos require more coherent prompts. Consider using multi-shot mode to break 15 seconds into 2-3 distinct segments with clear instructions for each.

### 6. Can I create vertical (9:16) videos for TikTok/Reels?

Yes. Select 9:16 aspect ratio before generating. For best results, upload a source image already cropped to vertical orientation, or use a square image and let the AI determine composition.

### 7. How do I make it more "cinematic"?

Three techniques that reliably add cinematic quality:
1. **Shallow depth of field**: Add "shallow DOF" or "cinematic bokeh" to prompts
2. **Controlled camera movement**: Use push-in or pull-back rather than static
3. **Lighting language**: Add "golden hour" / "soft directional light" / "natural light shift"

### 8. How do I add music or voiceover reliably?

For music: Upload a 3-30 second MP3/WAV file in the audio settings. Audio starts at frame 1 and trims automatically if longer than your video.

For voiceover: Generate the video first, then add voiceover in a video editor. Current AI video models do not support lip-sync from custom audio.

### 9. Which Wan 2.6 mode should I use (I2V vs T2V vs R2V)?

| Mode | Full Name | Use Case |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **I2V** | Image-to-Video | You have a photo you want to animate (this tutorial) |
| **T2V** | Text-to-Video | You want to generate video from a text description only |
| **R2V** | Reference-to-Video | You have a reference video style you want to replicate |

For photo animation, always use **I2V** (Image-to-Video).

### 10. Why might my results look different from the examples?

AI generation involves controlled randomness. Even with identical prompts, results vary between generations. Factors that affect output:
- Source image quality and composition
- Specific wording choices in prompts
- Selected duration and resolution
- Current model version and parameters

If results are consistently poor, the issue is likely your source image or prompt structure, not bad luck.
