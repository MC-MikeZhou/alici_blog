Imagine how much you could learn if you deeply studied 1,000 [MrBeast](https://www.linkedin.com/company/mrbeastyoutube?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention) thumbnails.

Now triple the thumbnails and remove the time barrier. Yeah, you’re in for a treat.

I studied nearly 3,000 MrBeast thumbnails and 600+ tests across 125 of his videos from 2020-2025. Here’s what I learned…

* * *

Quick Understanding of the Tests:
---------------------------------

![Image 4: Article content](https://media.licdn.com/dms/image/v2/D4E12AQFDpS-3vE4crA/article-inline_image-shrink_400_744/B4EZWUZgGNH0AY-/0/1741951468840?e=2147483647&v=beta&t=o8Qbw4bcnTcralAnW1rHfytk9wd1BTGwjQ2hns9WdUY)

Quick glance at my spreadsheet

I used [Viewstats](https://www.linkedin.com/company/viewstats?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-mention), YouTube's API, and a massive Google spreadsheet to accumulate these results. I tracked the test period, # of tests, results, and views. I don't have access to MrBeast's direct results, so I make evidence-based assumptions based on what I see on YouTube.

A "test" is a single change on a video, such as a direct thumbnail change (not A/B), an A/B test, or a title change. One singular A/B test includes two to three thumbnails.

Across the 125 videos I studied, MrBeast ran:

*   304 thumbnail changes
*   322 A/B tests
*   30 title changes

By the way, in case you don't know or trust me yet, just know that I study thumbnails for a living and [break them down weekly](https://www.linkedin.com/in/josiah-hritsko/?trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block) on my LinkedIn.

* * *

MrBeast Tested 4 Major Changes That Made Him Update Old Thumbnails.
-------------------------------------------------------------------

While MrBeast ran 600+ tests, a lot of those were to find results for individual videos.

These are the four major patterns I found that affected more than just one video. The results from 1 and 2caused him to change many old video thumbnails.

### Change #1: Open vs Closed Mouth.

![Image 5: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

This is his most well-known test that ran in September 2023, when he got access YouTube’s Test and Compare feature. He found that closed mouth thumbnails earned a higher watch time share.

### Change #2: Updating Jimmy’s Face.

![Image 6: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

After losing weight and gaining muscle, MrBeast’s physique changed over time. So on June 29, 2024, he changed thumbnails to an updated face.

### Change #3: General Cleanup.

![Image 7: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

On July 2, 2024 he changed 40+ thumbnails. Admittedly, the only thing I found that changed was the quality of thumbnails. This was either do to a YouTube update or general back catalog cleanup.

### Change #4: For You Widget.

![Image 8: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

A few months ago, some users got access to a beta feature that showed a “New” widget on new videos. The feature never materialized. But, since that time, creators have been testing different widgets in the upper right-hand corner of thumbnails, such as “New” and “Trending.” MrBeast tested four thumbnails with “For You” on February 17, 2025. They all lost.

* * *

A Few Patterns Emerged in His Testing Days, Dates, Times, & # of Tests.
-----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Tuesday was the Most Popular Testing Day

His most popular testing days were Tuesday (166 tests), Saturday (140 tests), and Monday (144 tests). Honestly, I was surprised he didn’t test more on Saturday to match with his new video uploads.

![Image 9: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

### He Tested More on 3 Specific Dates

The dates with the most tests were June 29, 2024 (56 tests), when he tested face changes; July 2, 2024 (48 tests), when he did a general thumbnail cleanup; and October 17, 2023 (30 tests), when ViewStats seemingly started collecting A/B test data.

### His Testing Time Range Varied

He tested anywhere from day-of-upload to 4 years after upload. But since October 2023, when beta A/B testing rolled out to most creators, the majority of his tests take place within the first week of a video’s release date (128 in the first week vs 119 after a week)

![Image 10: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

### He Ran More Tests Per Video When A/B Testing Launched

His average tests per fall 2023 videos is 12, for 2024 videos is ~7, and for 2025 videos is ~4. The range per video was anywhere from 1 test to 34 tests.

When A/B testing released in fall 2023, he went full speed ahead with testing those videos. He ran 21 tests on “$1 vs $100,000,000 Car!,” 22 on “$1 vs $500,000 Plane Ticket!,” and 27 on “World’s Deadliest Laser Maze!” These are his most-tested videos while his average is ~5 tests per video. Coincidentally, Plane Ticket and $1 Car are also two of his most-viewed videos in the past two years.

Sometimes it takes 20+ tests to achieve your most-viewed video. And sometimes it takes only 1 test, like Airrack's most-viewed video ([breakdown on that video here](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-airrack-broke-44m-views-his-most-viral-video-josiah-hritsko-c71he/?trackingId=%2FaNQqd7dQI%2BIjcIyEmEsWA%3D%3D&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block)).

* * *

Certain Visual Patterns Won Over Others. Here's How AI Analyzed the Results:
----------------------------------------------------------------------------

![Image 11: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

If you follow my LinkedIn, you know I'd consider MrBeast's thumbnails to be "well-done" on my [Thumbnail Doneness Scale](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josiah-hritsko_the-thumbnail-doneness-scale-activity-7300864325634646017-8dzH?utm_source=share&utm_medium=member_desktop&rcm=ACoAABXopaEBGIxn-FFPafBIWbAAhabY--smppo&trk=article-ssr-frontend-pulse_little-text-block). But there are other visual patterns that emerged when I studied all of these.

I uploaded my spreadsheet to ChatGPT and asked to analyze for visual patterns. Here’s what I found.

### MrBeast's Face

MrBeast’s face is nearly always present. Excluding, “Press This Button To Win $100,000!,” “I Gave My Credit Card To Random People,” and “Offering People $100,000 To Quit Their Job” Jimmy’s face is always on a thumbnail.

### Big Expressions

Closed mouth expressions won more than open-mouthed. Natural, mid-action facial expressions won in posed vs candid tests. Occasionally unusual expressions won over traditional excited faces.

### Action Poses

People in action poses (jumping, running, reacting) outperformed those in static poses.

Backgrounds: Thumbnails with blurred bgs, a sharp foreground-background contrast, and dark bgs with bright foregrounds won.

### Primary Colors

Red & green & yellow were the most common colors in winning thumbnails. Red (dangerous challenges, high stakes moments); green (money). Simpler, high contrast thumbnails won over dramatic lighting filters.

### Arrows & Circles (Mostly in 2020)

Arrows & circles were frequent for directing a viewer’s eye to objects (money, people, boxes, etc), specifically in the earlier years around 2020.

### Zoomed-In Scale

Winning thumbnails zoomed in more on the important elements (face, cash, important objects).

### Specific Numbers

Most titles based around money included a large dollar amount.. Also common numbers on thumbnails were: 37, 54, 97, 94, 150, 5, 6, and 2.

He Used Past Thumbnails to Inform Current Thumbnails.
-----------------------------------------------------

![Image 12: Article content](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/i-studied-3000-mrbeast-thumbnails-heres-what-learned-josiah-hritsko-hkyae)

Three days before MrBeast released “World’s Deadliest Laser Maze!,” he tested laser thumbnail color variants on “Steal This $100,000 Diamond, You Keep It.”

In a red vs blue vs green test, red seemingly won.

So instead of guessing which color would work for his new video, MrBeast used that new knowledge to launch with red lasers.

Then in round six, he retested the colors nine times. He actually saw varying results with blue winning and then green.

5 Major Takeaways:
------------------

*   Don’t be afraid to test. Your back catalog is a playground, and you can keep learning from it.
*   Run large-scale tests all at once on your back catalog to skyrocket your knowledge.
*   Use the first seven days as a good starting point for most tests. But also try testing videos older than a year old if they’re evergreen and still getting views.
*   Test each video as much as it needs to be tested. Not every video needs 20 A/B tests. But others do.
*   Find what visual patterns resonate with your audience. Blurred backgrounds, big faces, and action poses work for MrBeast, but what works for you?
